§ 01 — Company Overview

Who Is Superhuman?

Superhuman Platform Inc. is the company that used to be Grammarly Inc. In October 2025 it took the name of the email client it had bought three months earlier, and now sells a four-product suite — Grammarly (writing), Superhuman Mail (email), Superhuman Docs (the successor to Coda) and Superhuman Go (a cross-app assistant and agent runtime). Its pitch is that most AI tools work in isolation, which creates a "context gap" where users re-explain their work in every app. Everything in this report is a test of whether the company can actually close that gap with data, not slides.

The reason to study it through a data-platform lens: Superhuman is simultaneously (a) one of the few consumer AI companies running a custom model at 200K+ queries per second, (b) a Databricks lakehouse customer for roughly five years, and (c) a serial acquirer of products that each ship with their own datastore, identity model and permission scheme. The interesting question is not whether the pieces are good. It is whether they are one platform.

Core Thesis Superhuman has built a world-class platform for observing and serving one model (the writing assistant) and is now trying to build a second, very different platform for grounding agents in customers' own work — mostly by acquisition (Coda Brain, Mail, Rows, Fathom, GPTZero). The first is proven and publicly documented. The second is where the business case lives, and the public evidence for it is thin: agent evals, trace ingestion and a unified permission graph appear on a roadmap, not in production, in the company's own partner write-up.

§ 02 — Market Analysis

Industry Landscape & Competitive Positioning

Market Context

The AI productivity market has stopped being a feature race and become a context race. The assistant that knows what you wrote, who you met and what is permitted to be shared wins the workflow. Three forces define 2026: platform owners are shipping the data layer themselves (Microsoft's "Work IQ" grounds Copilot in mail, files, meetings and chats); connector standards like MCP are commoditising integrations; and pricing is moving from seats to usage, which exposes anyone who cannot meter cost per agent action.

The Incumbent Benchmark

Microsoft 365 Copilot reached 15M paid seats on the FQ2 2026 call (Jan 28, 2026), about 3.3% of a 450M+ commercial base. Even with default distribution, paid agentic adoption is early. REPORTED

The Context-Native Peers

Glean reported $200M ARR in Dec 2025 (doubled in nine months; $7.2B valuation). Notion is estimated at $865M ARR in July 2026, +82% YoY, now positioned around agents rather than apps. ESTIMATES

The last public revenue figure — "$700M+" — dates from May 2025, about 17 months ago. There is no 2026 update. Treat growth claims as unverified. STALE

Industry Trends Shaping Superhuman's Fate

🧠

Context Becomes the Moat

Model quality is converging; what differs is what the assistant is allowed to see and remember. That makes permission-aware retrieval and durable memory the core engineering problem, not a feature.

🔌

MCP Commoditises Connectors

Docs and Mail both expose MCP servers so ChatGPT, Claude and Cursor can reach in. Good for distribution; bad for lock-in. A Metrigy analyst's read: the challenge is giving customers enough value that they prefer Superhuman's own UI.

💳

Seats Give Way to Usage

Notion went usage-based in May 2026. Coda AI credits were paused Jul 6, 2026 and slated to return around August. Metering per agent action is now a data-platform requirement.

✍️

Writing Assistance Is Commoditised

Grammarly's original wedge (grammar) is now a baseline LLM capability. Its remaining edge is distribution (a "1M+ apps and websites" footprint) and 15+ years of accept/reject telemetry.

Key Success Factors in This Domain

Permissioned Context at Scale

Answer only from what this user may see, with citations, at low latency. The hard part is staleness: access revoked after indexing.

Distribution at the Point of Work

Being in the text box beats being in a destination app. Superhuman's extension footprint is its real asset.

Cost-to-Serve Discipline

Inference spend must be attributable per tenant and per agent action, or usage pricing becomes guesswork.

Trust & Rights Governance

Provenance, consent and opt-out for any person, persona or content the system touches. The Expert Review suit shows the price of getting this wrong.

Where Superhuman Is Struggling

Rights governance failure

Expert Review attributed AI-generated feedback to named real experts (some deceased) without consent. Class action filed Mar 11, 2026 alleging damages above $5M; feature pulled the same day. CEO: "The feature was not a good feature." Company calls the claims meritless.

Pricing trust

A June 30, 2026 email told some free Coda users "your free plan is about to feel smaller" with a Jul 6 upgrade deadline. Staff later said no existing limits were being cut, and that the email "may not have come across as we hoped."

Disclosure gap

No revenue update in 17 months; a $320M Form D with the business-combination box ticked but no named deal; 5 acquisitions in ~21 months. Investors and customers are being asked to trust integration they cannot see.

Evidence limits

I found no usable Reddit threads on Go or Docs. Customer sentiment here comes from the company's own community forum, which skews toward existing Coda users. Treat sentiment as thin.

Part B — The Data Platform Teardown

Superhuman's data estate, reconstructed from a customer case study, a conference-style engineering write-up, a partner blog, a trust center, product launch posts and press. It is best read as seven layers. The first three are mature and well-documented; the last four are where the story gets harder to see.

Databricks Delta Lake Unity Catalog Spark Structured Streaming Databricks Model Serving vLLM Databricks SQL Tableau AWS Kubernetes NVIDIA H100 / L40S Snowflake Cortex (Coda Brain) Turbopuffer Fivetran Google Cloud OpenAI Iterable

Red pills = documented as core to a named workload. Gray pills = named in a source but with an unstated or inferred role. Absence from this list is not evidence of absence.

1 · Analytics Lakehouse
Databricks on AWS, Delta Lake at the core, Unity Catalog for governance CONFIRMED

A 2022 customer story describes replacing a homegrown analytics system (an in-house SQL-like DSL, always-on Amazon EMR clusters, spreadsheet copy-paste for ad-spend data, no BI support) with a single lakehouse serving ETL, analytics and ML. Tableau sits on top for executive reporting; Databricks SQL serves marketing, product and BI. Unity Catalog supplies role-based access, lineage and data-quality monitoring. Reported outcomes: daily events available in under 15 minutes versus 4 hours; ingestion at about 10% of the prior warehouse cost; ~5 billion app events per day. Snowflake was evaluated and rejected for weak data-science/ML support, unpredictable cost at scale, and less control over data ownership. The page was modified as late as Sept 16, 2026 and now carries an "Agent Bricks" product tag, but the text does not describe its use.

2 · Event Store
6,000+ event types, one Delta table each, medallion layers CONFIRMED (2023)

The replacement for the bespoke "Gnar" lake ingests events from 40 internal and external clients; a Spark Structured Streaming job routes each event type to its own table. Bronze holds raw history, silver the conformed view for self-service and ML, gold the read-optimised models. Schema enforcement was introduced deliberately; data-delivery time fell about 94%. MY READ One table per event type keeps typed telemetry clean, but it means 6,000+ objects to govern, and every new agent action type adds more. It also says nothing about how agent traces (multi-step, nested, content-bearing) are stored. That is a different data shape.

3 · Model Training & Serving
Custom writing LLM at 200K+ QPS on Databricks Model Serving VENDOR-REPORTED (May 8, 2026)

A custom grammatical-error-correction LLM serves real-time suggestions at peak above 200K QPS (~50 input and ~50 output tokens per request) on 250+ GPUs, against SLOs of p99 under 1 second and 99.99% availability, with zero quality regression on Superhuman's evaluation harnesses. It replaced a self-managed vLLM stack on L40S GPUs that needed months of manual tuning per model iteration. Databricks engineering added power-of-two-choices load balancing via a custom endpoint-discovery service, concurrency-based autoscaling (aggressive up, conservative down), lazy-loading container images (startup from minutes to seconds), FP8 quantisation (up to ~30% per-pod gain), a multiprocessing RPC server (~20%), and C++/async scheduling work. Target: 750–1,200 QPS per H100 pod, a 60% gain. Superhuman keeps ownership of training, quantisation and quality bars.

What is conspicuously absent: the write-up does not name Unity Catalog, MLflow or Delta for this workload. Its "future direction" lists experiment tracking, evaluation across classical ML and GenAI/agents, a model and LLM-judge registry, and agent-trace ingestion — i.e., the lifecycle and observability layer is still to be moved onto the platform.

4 · Context & Retrieval
Coda Brain: Packs → retrieval stage → generation stage, on Snowflake Cortex CONFIRMED (Coda's own post)

Coda's description: data syncs in via Packs (500+ public, plus custom; 30% of active docs used Packs; 4B+ rows synced in a year). Each prompt is split into a retrieval stage that determines what the user may access and a generation stage that writes the answer. Per-user authentication mirrors source-system permissions. The post names Snowflake Cortex Search (indexing), Cortex Analyst (natural language to SQL) and Cortex LLMs, and exposes the backend to developers via API and SQL as a "pre-configured data warehouse." Go is described as built on Coda Brain.

MY READ This is the sharpest tension in the stack. Superhuman's data team chose Databricks over Snowflake in 2022 for ML support, cost predictability and ownership. Its flagship context product ran on Snowflake. Whether Coda Brain has been re-platformed since the merger is UNKNOWN, as are retrieval latency, index freshness, chunking and embedding choices, and how access revocation propagates to already-indexed content.

5 · Agent Runtime & Connectors
Go + Agent Store + SDK + MCP CONFIRMED

Go connects to 100+ apps, orchestrates first- and third-party agents (Google Workspace, Outlook, Jira, Confluence, Salesforce connectors; partners such as Common Room, Fireflies, Quizlet), keeps memory of user context and past actions, asks for approval before acting, and refuses to operate in password managers or sensitive fields. Docs and Mail expose MCP servers (beta) so external assistants can read and write Superhuman data. Rows (live integrations, AI analyst) and Fathom (meeting capture, already a Go connector) are being folded in to deepen the structured and conversational context. The Agents SDK is in closed beta.

6 · Infra & Subprocessors
Multi-cloud, multi-vendor footprint INFERRED

The lakehouse story is explicitly AWS. The trust center I retrieved lists Turbopuffer, OpenAI, Iterable, Fivetran and Google Cloud as subprocessors without labelling roles. A reasonable reading: Google Cloud hosts at least the Mail product, Fivetran moves SaaS data into a warehouse, Turbopuffer provides vector search, OpenAI supplies third-party LLM calls. The list names neither Databricks, AWS nor Snowflake, so it cannot be the group-wide inventory — it is likely product-scoped. The sensible conclusion: a hybrid model strategy (a proprietary small model on the high-volume writing path, third-party frontier models for generation) over at least two clouds.

7 · Governance & Identity
Strong compliance posture, two permission models, gaps on residency CONFIRMED UNKNOWN

Certifications: SOC 2 Type 2, ISO/IEC 27001, 27017, 27018 and (trust center) 27701, with a SecurityScorecard "A". Stated principles: user content is not sold or monetised; users own what they write; third-party providers may not train on user content; one sign-in across the suite. Internally, access to analytics data is governed by Unity Catalog roles. Externally, agent retrieval is governed by replicating each source system's permissions through per-user auth. Those are two different permission paradigms with no published bridge. Not stated: data residency, retention periods, MCP security model, and whether Go's persistent memory is purged when access is revoked. Historical note: a 2018 extension flaw (CVE-2018-6654) exposed authentication tokens; it was hot-fixed within hours.

Critical Technical Gap Superhuman has the two things that are hard to buy — behavioural telemetry at planetary scale and the inference engineering to serve a model at 200K QPS. § 05 — PM Critique

Product Strategy Assessment: What's Real, What's Risk, What's Missing

Superhuman has executed well on what it can already do: it keeps a very large writing product fast, cheap and reliable, and it has assembled a credible set of context sources in 21 months. The critique below is about the distance between that and the claim on its homepage — that an assistant can safely act across your work. Nearly every gap is a data platform gap, which is why this report is organised around it.

⚠ Structural Risk
Three Data Estates, One Story
The public record shows a Databricks lakehouse on AWS for product analytics and ML serving, a Snowflake Cortex-based retrieval layer inside Coda Brain (which Go is built on), and a Google Cloud/Fivetran footprint for Mail. Superhuman's own data team rejected Snowflake in 2022 on ML support, cost predictability and ownership. If Coda Brain still runs on it, the company carries two warehouse engines, two permission models and two cost curves under a single "Go" brand, and every acquisition adds a third or fourth. This is manageable if deliberate. The risk is that it is accidental, and that nobody can currently answer "what did this agent know?" without querying three systems. A deliberate decision (consolidate or formalise) should be made now and published internally with a cost model.
◈ Strategic Gap
Agents Are Shipping Before the Instruments to Judge Them
The Databricks write-up lists experiment tracking, an LLM-judge registry and agent-trace ingestion as future work. Meanwhile Go launched with 100+ agents, including third-party agents from a Store, with an SDK in beta. For the writing model there is a mature eval harness ("zero quality regression"). For multi-step agents that read email and write to calendars there is no public equivalent. The first serious agent failure — a wrong send, a leaked draft — will have no trace to explain it. Evals and traces must precede, not follow, third-party distribution.
⚠ Structural Risk
Permission-Aware Retrieval Breaks at the Edges
Coda Brain's design — per-user auth and a retrieval stage that checks access — is the right shape. It is robust for live pulls. It is much harder for already-indexed content (access revoked after vectorisation) and for derived content: Go advertises persistent memory of user context and past actions. If an employee loses access to a Jira project, does Go's memory forget what it learned there? Nothing public says so. INFERRED This is where enterprise security reviews will focus, and "SOC 2" will not answer it.
◈ Strategic Gap
Rights and Provenance Are a Data Problem, Not a Policy Problem
Expert Review attributed AI-generated critiques to real, named experts (some deceased) with an easy-to-miss footer disclaimer. It drew a class action alleging damages above $5M and was disabled the same day. The CEO's reaction — "not a good feature" — and the stated plan for a persona library where experts are paid is the right instinct, but a persona marketplace needs a rights ledger: who consented, to what, with what attribution and payout, and which outputs used it. That is a data model with audit requirements. Without it, the next persona or "authenticity" feature (GPTZero joins the suite) repeats the same failure mode.
◈ Execution Gap
Pricing Moves Faster Than Cost Visibility
Go was free through Feb 1, 2026; Coda AI credits were paused on Jul 6, 2026 and slated to return around August; a free-tier email created confusion that staff had to correct. These are symptoms of a company still discovering its cost-to-serve per agent action. At ~100B LLM calls per week (reported), small per-call misestimates compound. Usage pricing needs per-tenant, per-agent metering as a first-class dataset, and a rule that finance and product read the same table.
⚠ Positioning Risk
The Moat Is Connector Count — and Connectors Are Being Commoditised
Superhuman can write in 1M+ places but read from about 100. MCP is collapsing that connector advantage for everyone: Superhuman itself exposes Docs and Mail via MCP so ChatGPT, Claude and Cursor can reach in. That helps distribution and hurts lock-in. The defensible position is not "we have connectors" but "we hold the permissioned, high-quality, cross-source context that others do not" — inbox plus meetings plus docs plus writing history. That is exactly the layer Microsoft's Work IQ targets.
⚠ Execution Risk
Integration Debt From Five Acquisitions in ~21 Months
Coda (Dec 2024), Mail (Jul 2025), Rows (Feb 2026), GPTZero (Jun 2026), Fathom (Sep 2026). Each brings its own datastore, identity, tenancy and data terms: workspaces, mailboxes, spreadsheets, student and educator submissions, meeting transcripts. Fathom customers "keep the same app, login and user experience," which is kind to customers and a signal that the identity layer is not yet unified. The $320M Form D (business combination box ticked) suggests more deals are coming. Reported revenue for the two acquisitions with figures (Mail, GPTZero) is about 9% of the base; the real value is data and capability, which makes data integration the actual deal thesis.
✦ Opportunity
The Accept / Reject Corpus Is the Most Underused Asset
Superhuman has more than a decade of labelled preference signals — which suggestions people accepted, rejected or ignored, ~5B events/day at the 2022 snapshot. The custom writing model and its evaluation harnesses already use this kind of feedback. The unexploited step is applying the same loop to agent actions: every Go proposal that a user approves, edits or rejects is a reward signal for agent quality. Few competitors have both a mature feedback pipeline and a surface where the user is already deciding. Handled with strict content-free event design, it is a durable evaluation advantage.
✦ Opportunity
Use the Databricks Relationship as a Governance Accelerator
Databricks and Superhuman co-engineered the serving platform (eight Databricks authors on the write-up), and the vendor's own list of next steps matches Superhuman's gaps: experiment tracking, evaluation, a judge registry, trace ingestion. The customer page was updated in September 2026 with an Agent Bricks tag. Pulling those pieces forward and making Unity Catalog the governance plane for all internal data, including acquired products, is the fastest path to a single answer to "what did this agent know?" The trade-off is deeper vendor concentration, which should be a conscious choice with an exit plan.

§ 07 — Mock Product Strategy

Product Strategy 2026–2028: From Writing Telemetry to a Governed Context Platform

Document Type This is a mock product strategy document written from the perspective of a Senior PM for the data platform at Superhuman. It is grounded in public information but represents analytical recommendations, not Superhuman's internal roadmap. All targets are illustrative hypotheses.
01

Strategic Vision & North Star

Vision: Every action a Superhuman agent takes can answer four questions — what did you know, who allowed it, what did it cost, and was it good? — in seconds, for the user, the admin and the auditor.

North Star Metric: Governed Action Rate — the share of agent actions (a sent draft, a booked meeting, an updated doc) that (a) cite at least one permission-checked source, (b) have a complete trace in the evaluation store, and (c) are accepted without edit. Current baseline: not measurable from public evidence, and probably not measurable internally either, which is the point. Target by end of 2027: 60%.

The Strategic Pivot: From "an assistant that is in every app" to "the assistant whose actions are explainable and billable." Reach is already won; trust and unit economics are not.

02

Three Strategic Bets (2026–2028)

Bet 1: One Context Plane

Create a single identity, entity and permission service used by Go, Docs, Mail, Fathom and GPTZero. Every agent read goes through a policy check that returns allowed / denied, evidence and expiry. Internally, make Unity Catalog the governance plane for all analytical data, including that of acquired products. Make an explicit, costed decision on the retrieval engine: re-platform Coda Brain off Snowflake Cortex or formalise a dual-engine design.

Why This First Everything else — evals, metering, rights, enterprise sales — depends on being able to say who a user is across products and what they may see. It is also the only bet that gets harder with every acquisition.

Bet 2: Agent Evaluation & Observability Fabric

Pull forward the items Superhuman's own partner post listed as future: trace ingestion, experiment tracking, an LLM-judge registry and offline replay. Add per-tenant, per-agent cost metering as a first-class dataset.

Phase 1 (Q4 2026): Trace Everything

Every Go agent step logged with inputs referenced (not copied), policy decisions, latency and token cost. Content-free by default; content access requires a flagged debug path.

Phase 2 (Q1–Q2 2027): Judge & Replay

LLM-judge registry with versioned rubrics; replay any trace against a new model or prompt; regression gates for agent releases, mirroring the writing model's "zero regression" bar.

Phase 3 (Q3 2027): Scorecards

Publish quality, latency and cost scorecards to Store and SDK partners; rank agents on them; make badges a condition of enterprise visibility.

Moat Mechanism

Every approve / edit / reject on an agent action becomes labelled data, the same loop that built the writing product, now applied to agents.

Bet 3: Rights & Provenance Ledger

A consent, attribution and payout system for any real person, persona or third-party content the product uses, including the planned expert persona library. It doubles as an enterprise audit export ("what was used in this output and under what terms") and underpins GPTZero-style authenticity features.

Commercial Angle Package the ledger, audit exports and published residency/retention options as an enterprise "Trust" tier. Illustrative: if 5% of the 50,000 stated organisations adopt a $5/seat/month uplift at 100 seats each, that is about $15M annually (2,500 orgs × 100 seats × $5 × 12). Small against $700M, but it is a sales unlock for regulated buyers, not primarily a revenue line.

06

Strategic Don'ts (What to Stop or Avoid)

Don't acquire another data-bearing product before Context Plane v1

Five deals in 21 months is already a lot of integration debt. A sixth with its own identity and datastore makes the unification project harder than it is today.

Don't relaunch usage credits without cost data

A second reversal costs more trust than the first. Price from measured cost per action, then publish the logic.

Don't ship named-person features without a consent ledger

The Expert Review lawsuit exists because the feature came before the rights model. Reverse the order.

Don't market "1M+ apps" as the context moat

It measures where Superhuman can write, not what it can read. Lead with the number of permissioned read sources and the quality of answers drawn from them.

§ 08 — The Verdict

The Verdict

Superhuman has the rare combination of what is hard to buy: telemetry at planetary scale, inference engineering that holds up at 200K+ QPS, and distribution inside the text box. Its analytics lakehouse is a decisive 2022 call that has aged well. On that evidence, the company is better prepared for AI than almost any other software business of its size.

But the bet it has made — that permissioned, cross-source context is the moat — depends on a layer that the public record does not show. The context layer descends from an acquisition, runs (at least as described) on an engine its own data team rejected, and is surrounded by controls that are still on a roadmap: agent evals, trace ingestion, a rights ledger, cost metering, a unified permission model. The recent evidence of strain — the Expert Review suit, the pricing reversals, five integrations in under two years, a 17-month-old revenue disclosure — is what a company looks like when its product ambition has outrun its governance data model.

None of that is fatal. All of it is fixable with the same discipline the company applied to its writing model: shadow-test, set SLOs, require zero regression. The next 12 months will show whether Superhuman applies that discipline to agents before the first serious failure applies it for them.

Bottom Line Superhuman is a data-rich company with a data-governance gap exactly where its strategy needs strength. The fix is not more connectors or more acquisitions; it is one context plane, one trace store, one cost ledger and one rights ledger. The window to build them is the time it takes Microsoft and Google to make their own context layers good enough that no one needs a third.
